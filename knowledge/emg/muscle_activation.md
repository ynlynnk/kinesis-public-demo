# Understanding muscle activation

## Plain-language explanation

Muscle activation describes the electrical activity associated with a muscle being recruited during a task.

Surface EMG can help show:

- when recorded muscle activity begins;
- when activity decreases;
- periods of relatively higher or lower activity;
- repeated activation patterns; and
- the timing of activation relative to movement.

Muscle activation is related to muscle effort, but it is not a direct measurement of muscle strength or force.

## Interpreting activation levels

Within the same recording, relatively higher EMG activity may indicate that:

- more motor units were recruited;
- active motor units increased their firing activity;
- the task required more muscular effort; or
- the signal was affected by movement or recording conditions.

These possibilities cannot always be distinguished from the graph alone.

Relatively lower EMG activity may indicate:

- reduced recruitment;
- a less demanding phase of the exercise;
- a change in movement strategy;
- relaxation between repetitions; or
- a sensor or signal-quality issue.

Do not automatically describe higher activation as better and lower activation as worse.

## Activation is not the same as force

EMG amplitude and muscle force may be related under controlled conditions. However, the relationship can differ depending on:

- the muscle;
- contraction type;
- joint angle;
- movement speed;
- exercise direction;
- fatigue;
- sensor placement; and
- the individual being assessed.

Therefore, KINESIS must not convert normalised EMG activation directly into muscle force or strength.

## KINESIS activation graph

KINESIS calculates a combined activation pattern from the processed EMG channels and normalises the values within the selected session.

Use the graph to describe:

- when relative activation increased or decreased;
- whether activation appeared in repeated patterns;
- whether an activation change occurred near a movement change; and
- whether an unusual spike or flat section may need review.

Do not use the graph to make an absolute comparison of activation magnitude between separately normalised sessions.

## Comparing EMG with IMU movement

When the EMG and IMU patterns are displayed together:

- EMG represents relative muscle-associated electrical activity;
- IMU represents relative physical movement;
- similar timing may suggest that activation occurred with movement;
- different timing may require closer review; and
- neither signal alone explains the complete exercise technique.

Use terms such as:

- "The activation and movement patterns increased at approximately the same time."
- "Muscle activation appeared before the main movement."
- "Movement continued while recorded activation decreased."
- "The timing difference may need physiotherapist review."

Do not state that timing differences are abnormal without professional review.

## Activation and fatigue

Increasing activation does not automatically mean fatigue.

During some tasks, the body may recruit additional motor units to maintain performance. In other situations, changes may be caused by movement technique, contraction type, or recording conditions.

Use Model 1's fatigue result for the AI fatigue classification. Treat the activation graph only as supporting evidence.

## What the physiotherapist should review

Consider:

1. Whether the recorded activity corresponds with the expected exercise period.
2. Whether repeated movements have reasonably consistent activation timing.
3. Whether activation and movement changes occur together.
4. Whether unexpected spikes or missing activity suggest a signal issue.
5. Whether the same exercise and recording method were used when comparing sessions.
6. Whether the AI interpretation agrees with professional observation.

## Safe explanation examples

Use:

- "The graph shows relatively higher activation during this part of the session."
- "The muscle-activity pattern appears to repeat with the movement."
- "The signals changed at different times and may need review."
- "The graph represents normalised activation, not measured strength."
- "The cause of this difference cannot be determined from the graph alone."

Avoid:

- "This muscle is stronger."
- "The patient used exactly twice as much force."
- "High activation proves that the exercise was performed correctly."
- "Low activation confirms muscle weakness."
- "This pattern confirms an injury."

## Important limitation

KINESIS results support professional review and are not a medical diagnosis.

The activation graph is supporting information for system testing. It is not a measurement of clinical strength and is not a medical diagnosis.
