# Surface EMG basics

## What surface EMG measures

Surface electromyography, or sEMG, records electrical activity associated with muscle activation using electrodes placed on the skin.

The signal can help describe when and how strongly a measured muscle appears to be active during a task.

Surface EMG does not directly measure:

- pain;
- muscle strength;
- joint movement;
- a medical diagnosis; or
- the person's perceived effort.

## How to interpret the KINESIS EMG graph

The KINESIS session graph displays normalised EMG activation over time.

The displayed values range from 0 to 1 within the selected session:

- values closer to 1 represent relatively higher recorded activation within that session;
- values closer to 0 represent relatively lower recorded activation within that session.

These values are not raw electrical voltage.

Because each session is normalised separately, the graph should primarily be used to understand the pattern within a session. A value of 0.8 in one session should not automatically be treated as identical to 0.8 in another session.

A high graph value does not automatically mean that fatigue, pain, excessive effort, or incorrect movement occurred.

A low graph value does not automatically mean that the muscle was weak or inactive.

## Factors affecting the signal

Surface EMG recordings can be affected by:

- electrode position;
- electrode orientation;
- spacing between electrodes;
- skin-electrode contact;
- movement of an electrode or cable;
- nearby muscle activity, known as crosstalk;
- electrical noise;
- the muscle being measured; and
- differences in the exercise being performed.

Consistent recording procedures are important when comparing sessions.

## Signal-quality warning signs

Possible signal-quality concerns include:

- an unexpectedly flat signal;
- abrupt isolated spikes;
- sudden baseline changes;
- long missing sections;
- a pattern that does not correspond with the exercise timing; or
- a major difference that may be explained by changed sensor placement.

If the signal appears unreliable, the result should be marked as uncertain or interpreted cautiously.

## Relationship to fatigue

Changes in EMG amplitude or frequency can occur during a fatiguing task. However, no single change proves that clinically meaningful fatigue occurred.

The KINESIS fatigue classification comes from Model 1's analysis of the session windows. The displayed normalised EMG graph is supporting information and should not replace the model output or physiotherapist review.

## Relationship to movement

EMG represents muscle-associated electrical activity, while the IMU represents physical movement.

The two signals may be viewed together to understand timing. They should not be assumed to measure the same thing.

For example:

- increased EMG activity may occur with little visible movement;
- movement may occur through several muscles not represented by one EMG channel; and
- sensor noise may create apparent activity that requires review.

## Safe explanation examples

Use:

- "This graph shows relative muscle activation during the selected session."
- "The peak represents higher activation relative to other parts of this recording."
- "The EMG and movement signals appear to change at a similar time."
- "The signal may need review because an unexpected spike is present."
- "Sensor placement and recording conditions should be considered."

Avoid:

- "The graph proves that the muscle is damaged."
- "A higher value means the patient has more severe fatigue."
- "The EMG graph alone confirms that the exercise was correct."
- "The patient is stronger because this session has a higher normalised peak."

## Important limitation

KINESIS results support professional review and are not a medical diagnosis.

The surface EMG visualisation provides supporting information for system testing. It is not a medical diagnosis and does not replace professional assessment.
